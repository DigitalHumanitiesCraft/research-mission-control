# Workshop process walkthrough

This authored teaching walkthrough connects the [material-rule change](change-case.md) to observable work, messages and document revisions. It supplies the common example for the process simulation, the method model and the manuscript discussion. It is not a transcript or an evaluation. The supplied workshop and worksheet already contain the correct restricted wording. The missed passage, review, correction, acceptance and resumed session below are additional fictional events, not reported history of those files.

## Assignment and source boundary

Prepare the fictional workshop “Traceable project handovers” from R1 and R4. R5 subsequently prohibits original project material, including anonymized, redacted and paraphrased excerpts, throughout every workshop activity. Research maintains RQ-06 against R5. Workshop Operations owns workshop.md. Materials Operations owns worksheet.md and model-handover.md. Verification examines submitted revisions without modifying them and sends its complete initial finding first to the project owner under R3. H-01 remains unanswered and continues to block final printing and case-sheet allocation.

All source passages and baseline/restricted wording come from [records](records.md) and [the before/after fixtures](change-case.md#wording-changes). The simulation introduces no real participant data or external effects. Activating a simulation control grants no authority over a real project.

## Events and inspectable changes

1. The initial workspace contains the baseline permission from R1 and draft exercise and worksheet passages. Show the invitation to bring or paste an anonymized project excerpt. This is historical baseline wording, not the current permission.
2. R5 becomes available. Pause for the visitor to apply the replacement in the illustration. Display the source restriction and the documents it affects. This pause is a teaching control, not a method requirement to seek permission before interpreting an already governing source. Advancing simulated time alone must not apply a reserved action.
3. Research replaces RQ-06 with “Admit only wholly fictional workshop material and remove invitations to supply original content.” Preserve R1 as superseded history and cite R5 as the current ground. The knowledge revision is distinct from downstream artifact completion.
4. Research issues separate bounded briefs. Workshop Operations may change the exercise and facilitator briefing in workshop.md. Materials Operations may change worksheet.md and model-handover.md. Both read requirements.md and records.md, preserve retained requirements and keep H-01 open. A message displays sender, recipient, source revision, allowed writes and requested checks.
5. The operations contexts work in parallel. Show their separate activity tracks and the changing passages. The exercise changes from “Bring an anonymized excerpt from your project and use it as the source for your handover.” to “Use only Case U or Case C, revision training-v2, supplied in the training source.” The worksheet has an authored incomplete intermediate revision which still says “Paste your anonymized project excerpt and record its original location.” Mark it as a draft defect in this illustration.
6. Operations submits the concrete intermediate revisions with their checks and limits to Verification. Research receives a result notice. Submission does not mark the result as verified or accepted. The stated completion claim is visible separately from the artifact that supports or contradicts it.
7. Verification compares the submitted worksheet passage with R5. It records a correction-required finding identifying that exact invitation and explaining why anonymization fails the new rule. The complete finding goes first to the project owner. Verification changes no artifact.
8. Pause for the visitor acting as the fictional owner to route the finding to Materials Operations for a bounded correction. Playback cannot silently route it. Workshop Operations retains its already prepared revision. The finding's routing changes no acceptance state.
9. Materials Operations replaces the residual invitation with “Use only Case U or Case C, revision training-v2. Original project content is prohibited, including anonymized, redacted and paraphrased excerpts.” Show the old and new passages, the changed revision and the responsible writer. Preserve the worksheet's source, state, uncertainty and decision fields.
10. Verification rechecks the corrected revision against R5 and the retained criteria. Its complete new finding goes first to the project owner, applies to that revision and states the inspected scope. It does not erase the failed earlier submission and does not itself establish human acceptance, workshop delivery or learning outcomes.
11. Pause for explicit illustrative material acceptance by the visitor acting as owner. Acceptance is limited to the revised material. H-01 remains open. Neither playback nor a favorable finding may manufacture the owner's decision.
12. Research records the scoped decision, source and requirement references, result revision, findings and unresolved H-01 in maintained project knowledge. Operations remains responsible for its artifact files. A new illustrated session reads this recorded state and identifies what can continue and what still depends on H-01. This illustrates reconstruction from records, not automatic restoration of an earlier context window.

## State and interface contract

For each event, expose the responsible actor, input source or message, affected artifact and revision, observable action, resulting state, evidence and any required authority. A source-supported requirement, a prepared artifact, a submission, a finding and an acceptance decision are different objects. Replacing a revision requires examining the scope of previous findings rather than carrying their status forward automatically.

Use separate state indicators for assignment progress, artifact revision, verification finding and acceptance. A role can be idle while its result remains submitted. An unresolved owner decision does not block unrelated authorized work. The example deliberately preserves this distinction through H-01.

The interface puts the selected document and its passage changes at the center. Operator tracks reveal parallel work and waiting. A timeline opens previous snapshots and messages. Play, pause, previous, next and restart operate on authored browser state only. Decision gates stop playback. Messages that depend on an unresolved gate remain proposed and unsent until the visitor activates that gate. Returning before a gate invalidates later simulated choices, so replay cannot reuse acceptance for an earlier or changed revision. Repeated stepping must produce the same snapshot for the same path.

Animation marks a message transfer, changed passage or changed execution state. It does not depict hidden LLM reasoning, measured duration, real tool activity or autonomous execution. Reduced-motion mode preserves the same readable states and manual controls. Role definitions and provenance remain accessible without requiring the animation.

## Relationship to the paper and evaluation

This walkthrough is an executable explanation of the proposed contracts. It can support tasks such as identifying the writer of a correction, tracing a changed instruction to R5, distinguishing a favorable finding from acceptance, and resuming from recorded state while preserving H-01. Whether the representation improves understanding or whether the method improves research work remains untested. The manuscript's constructed scholarly synthesis case and proposed comparative evaluation retain their own scope.

## Adapt the example to your project

Start with one useful result and the sources that govern it. An exploratory project can use a provisional result, such as an evidenced comparison of interface approaches. Missing examples remain research questions and need not prevent initialization.

1. Replace the workshop objective with your intended result and an observable acceptance criterion. Identify what must be preserved from existing material.
2. Name the maintained source of requirements and decisions. Bind any external knowledge collection explicitly. Record who may update that knowledge and which sources remain read-only.
3. Choose write boundaries from actual artifacts. Split production only where the assignments can proceed independently. A small project may use one production context. Independent Verification still requires its own context and permitted return route.
4. Write a first bounded assignment with relevant sources, allowed changes, expected output and checks. Name what evidence the recipient must return and how receipt will be observed.
5. Reserve the decisions that actually need your judgement. Preserve unrelated open questions while allowing independent work. In this example, acceptance of corrected wording leaves workshop format unresolved.
6. Use the portable initializer for a supported new workspace, then inspect generated files, context activation and actual message delivery separately. After the first result, adjust responsibilities where observed work exposes an unnecessary handover or unclear ownership.

For a literature review, the governing source might be the agreed inclusion criterion, the writable artifact a selection register and the returned evidence the reasons for changed selections. A later session should reconstruct the current rule, examined revision, unresolved cases and allowed next action from maintained records. The workshop's role count and file names are examples rather than compulsory project structure.

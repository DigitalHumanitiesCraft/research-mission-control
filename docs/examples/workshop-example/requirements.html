Requirements for resuming a documented task¶

The authored case analysis yields a handover structure with a located source, a bounded next action and a visible decision boundary. R2 supplies a receiver's stated obstacles, while R1, R3–R5 supply explicit requirements. These records support the requirements below within the fictional scenario. They provide no evidence that this structure improves real project outcomes.

Analysis boundary¶

The unit of analysis is a stated need or instruction in R1–R6. The analyst groups those statements by the information a receiver needs and retains exact section references. The matrix is a completed authored example result. No coding exercise, interview study, participant test or independent analysis was performed.

Adopted scenario change¶

The maintained requirement RQ-06 follows restricted-v2. R1's earlier permission remains visible as historical scenario evidence. R5 controls every participant-facing instruction. The change record identifies the exact affected passages and their replacements.

Open contribution¶

H-01 belongs to the fictional workshop sponsor. Both case sheets and agenda variants are prepared. Printing and pair allocation remain pending. The matrix and the common worksheet can be inspected without resolving that choice. The unanswered choice supplies no evidence of a real sponsor, request or waiting team.

What a result check can establish¶

A reader can compare each matrix row with its named record, examine the worked handover and test whether the agenda totals 90 minutes. That establishes consistency of this authored package. Workshop suitability, learning and the sufficiency of these requirements in real handovers remain untested.

The classroom peer review under R4 is a learning activity. Feedback returns to the handover author for revision. It neither performs the separate independent Verification specified by R3 nor transfers acceptance authority to a participant. R3's full initial finding goes first to the human owner.
